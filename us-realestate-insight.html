01 · Introduction

Price depends on the home and the market around it

A large home may be expensive because of its size, or because it sits in a high-demand, high-income area. A model that only sees property features — bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage — can misattribute location effects to those features.

This dashboard compares a property-only baseline with a confounding-aware model that adds state, city and ZIP, then extends the analysis to affordability, investment screening and an interactive price estimator.

02 · Method

Four pages, from explanation to decision

1 · Model comparison. Sold properties are cleaned, missing values median-imputed with indicator flags, price and size log-transformed. Two LightGBM models are compared, and SHAP shows how feature importance shifts once geography is added.

2 · Geospatial analysis. Listings are joined with ACS state median household income to derive a price-income ratio, shown on a map with a price filter and state/city drill-down.

3 · Anomaly detection. Residuals (actual − predicted, normalized by predicted) flag underpriced homes below −10%, and a ZIP-level view combines average residual, underpriced share and price volatility.

4 · User input. Users enter location and property attributes to get a predicted fair price and, given an asking price, a simple buy/hold signal.

03 · Results

Location explains much of the price signal

Adding location lifts test R² from 0.291 to 0.814 and roughly halves RMSE and MAE.

Test metricNaïve (property only)Confounding-aware (+ location)
R²0.2910.814
RMSE$317,840$162,836
MAE$186,719$88,503

In SHAP, structural variables such as bathrooms and house size dominate the naïve model, while encoded ZIP and state features take over in the confounding-aware model — geography absorbs a large part of the pricing signal.

The affordability map makes it easy to see where housing is expensive relative to local income.

For screening, a predicted-versus-actual scatter and residual distribution surface homes that sold below model expectations (34.8% underpriced in the sample filter), and the ZIP-level view places them against market volatility.

04 · Discussion

Prediction and context work together

The visuals form a pipeline: the comparison shows geography matters, SHAP shows how the model uses it, and the residual and volatility views show where it can be used for screening. Future work includes ZIP- or tract-level socioeconomic data, richer recommendation scoring that accounts for uncertainty, and a more formal causal approach.
