01 · Overview

Can attention improve graph-conditioned floor plans?

Useful floor plans must be realistic, semantically correct, and topologically consistent, which makes them a natural testbed for structured generative modeling. This project reproduces a House-GAN++-style baseline on the public ResPlan dataset and extends it with GraphGPS-inspired attention, focusing on non-fixed completion: some rooms are given as constraints, and the rest must be generated so that local geometry and graph-level structure both stay plausible.

The work has three contributions: a full preprocessing pipeline from vector floor plans to graph-conditioned room-mask supervision, a curriculum that separates room generation from smaller door targets, and three architectural extensions: a GraphGPS generator, a hybrid GraphGPS with spatial message passing, and a pure transformer.

02 · Data

From vector polygons to graph-conditioned masks

RPLAN, used by the original House-GAN++, is not publicly accessible, so the project adopts ResPlan, a public vector-graph dataset with explicit connectivity. Each plan is normalized onto a 256 × 256 canvas, then rasterized into 64 × 64 binary masks per node. Nodes are rooms (living room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, balcony, storage) or openings (front door, interior door); edges encode adjacency or door-mediated connectivity.

A key finding came from error analysis: ResPlan often stores a room category, especially living rooms, as a MultiPolygon with tiny fragments. After rasterization these collapse into one-pixel slivers that look like doors, polluting the targets. Filtering them became a critical preprocessing step, effectively part of model design.

03 · Models

Local, global, or both

House-GAN++ baseline uses convolutional message passing (Conv-MPN) and a relational discriminator. GraphGPS replaces the local-only backbone with blocks that combine local convolutional message passing, pooled-token self-attention across nodes, and a feed-forward update broadcast back to the feature map.

Hybrid GraphGPS + Spatial CMP keeps House-GAN++’s spatial inductive bias while adding global attention. Early versions copied neighbor feature maps too directly, so the revised block uses degree-normalized pooling, positive-edge-only spatial exchange, and a small residual scale. A pure transformer treats each room as one token with graph structure as attention bias; it was not trained to convergence.

04 · Training

A curriculum from rooms to doors

Doors are small and sparse, so training every target jointly made the generator dominated by large room regions and unstable. The curriculum introduces difficulty progressively: Phase A1 rooms only, A2 adds the front door, A3 adds matched interior doors, and an optional Phase B enables adversarial refinement once supervised quality is acceptable.

Supervised phases minimize a weighted L1 loss (foreground weight 30) plus Dice and foreground-ratio terms on active nodes, and a smaller preservation loss on fixed rooms. Evaluation uses room IoU, active-node IoU, and the rates of empty and oversized predictions, alongside FID and GED for comparison with the House-GAN++ literature.

05 · Results

Higher IoU is not better geometry

On phase A, GraphGPS reaches a best validation room IoU of 0.3757 at epoch 20, and the hybrid 0.3494 at epoch 17. The hybrid drives the empty rate to zero earlier and produces more coherent local structure, while GraphGPS’s higher score reflects overlap-friendly but spatially noisy masks. Neither reaches the 0.45 room-IoU threshold for the next curriculum stage.

Three failure modes recur: non-fixed room blobs that are oversized rather than empty; spatial copying artifacts in early hybrids, fixed by normalized pooling; and adversarial instability, where the discriminator overpowers a generator that cannot yet produce basic rooms, which is why GAN refinement is deferred. Fixed nodes are always reconstructed reliably, but translating graph structure into stable spatial completions remains the bottleneck.

06 · Takeaways

Global attention is useful, spatial reasoning is necessary

Floor plan generation sits at the intersection of graph reasoning and spatial reasoning. Pooled-token attention captures relationships between rooms but lacks the spatial inductive bias to place them coherently, and rasterization artifacts in the data make preprocessing part of the model. The results should be read as conditional completion rather than unconstrained generation, since many layouts are valid for the same graph.

Next steps: geometry-aware supervision (center/size losses, overlap and contact constraints), relative positional encodings in the hybrid block, adversarial refinement once rooms and doors are reliable, and architecture-aware metrics such as connected-component counts, overlap rates, and door–room compatibility.

My contributions: ResPlan preprocessing, the graph construction pipeline, data cleaning and filtering, GraphGPS and Hybrid model implementation, curriculum training, quantitative evaluation, and failure analysis.
