01 · Introduction

AI as a role that can be deliberately designed

Generative AI is usually framed as efficiency or automation. This project instead treats the role of AI as something that can be designed, using drawing as an embodied activity where AI intervention directly shapes a designer’s actions and sense of authorship.

Drawing on Suchman’s situated action, Beer’s cybernetics and Rogers’ participatory interaction, it frames three roles for AI in creative systems — technical tool, creative assistant, and collaborative partner — and ends with a conceptual prototype built around controllability, feedback and traceability.

02 · Prototype

A visible, learnable AI participant

The prototype asks whether humans and AI can form an interaction that is perceptible, responsive and continuously negotiable. The AI is not an autonomous creator: it appears at key moments, and the user decides whether to accept, adjust or reject its response.

03 · System

Two hands, three phases

Bodily gesture is the main medium. The right hand switches modes — open palm for Paint, fist for Gesture Invocation — while the left hand executes: the index finger draws or records a gesture, and an open palm pauses or triggers.

1 · Initial Creation. Free gestural strokes become generative conditions and produce a first image, such as a mountain landscape.

2 · Annotation & Learning. The user selects a region they like; the system links its texture to the gesture that produced it.

3 · Gesture Invocation. Repeating a learned gesture recalls its texture onto the canvas, so the drawing grows through “Draw → Generate → Sample → Draw again” into a shared gestural language between human and AI.
